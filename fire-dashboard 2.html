Investment Accounts ?ISA, SIPP & GIA explainedISA — tax-free wrapper (£20k/yr allowance). Growth and withdrawals untaxed.
SIPP — pension you control. Tax relief on the way in, but locked until pension access age. 25% tax-free on withdrawal.
GIA — general investment account. No tax wrapper, but no restrictions either.
Each account can have its own growth rate to reflect different investment strategies.

Pensions ?DB & State PensionsDB (Defined Benefit) — a guaranteed annual income from a workplace scheme (e.g. NHS, civil service, teacher's). Check your annual benefit statement for the amount.
State Pension — currently £11,502/yr (2025/26) for full NI record. Check yours at gov.uk/check-state-pension. These reduce how much you need from your portfolio.

Bridge Risk ?The BridgeThe gap between early retirement and when your pensions start. During this period you rely entirely on your ISA, GIA, and cash. This is the riskiest phase of any FIRE plan — if markets crash here, you can't easily go back to work.
—

Financial Independence (FI) ?FI vs RE — they're different!FI = could you stop working today and your money lasts? Compares your current portfolio to what you'd need right now. You can be FI but choose to keep working.
—
Retire Early (RE) ?RE = Retire EarlyRE = will your projected portfolio at your target retirement age be enough? This factors in future contributions and growth. You can be on track for RE without being FI yet.

The Bridge ?Why this mattersThe bridge is the period between retiring early and your pensions starting. You must fund living costs from ISAs, GIAs, and cash alone. A market crash during this window is the single biggest risk to a FIRE plan because you're withdrawing, not contributing.
